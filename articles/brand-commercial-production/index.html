How a brand commercial is made: from brief to final cut

A clear guide to commercial production for brands: brief, concept, storyboard, production, edit, sound and colour — classic and AI-powered.

A brand commercial looks simple on screen: thirty seconds, a few shots, a logo at the end. Behind it is a production process that decides whether people remember the brand or scroll past. Here is how a commercial is made, step by step — and what changes when the footage is created with AI.

1. The brief

Everything starts with a short, honest brief: what the brand sells, who the audience is, what the commercial must achieve and where it will run — TV, YouTube, Instagram Reels, TikTok or a website. One clear goal is better than five. “Make people want to try our coffee at home” is a brief; “show everything about our company” is not.

2. Concept and script

The concept is the idea that carries the commercial: a situation, a feeling or a small story. The script turns it into scenes, lines and timing. Good commercials usually have one idea, one emotion and one clear moment for the product.

3. Storyboard and style frames

A storyboard shows each shot in order. Style frames show the look: light, colour, camera, mood. This is where the client sees the commercial before it exists — and where changes are cheap.

4. Production

In classic commercial production this is the shoot: locations, crew, cast, equipment, permits and the weather. With AI video production, the footage is generated instead: every shot is created, reviewed and regenerated until it matches the storyboard. The director’s job does not disappear — it moves from managing a set to directing every frame.

5. Edit, sound and colour

The edit gives the commercial its rhythm. Then come voice-over, music, sound design and colour grading. Sound is half of the impression: the same picture feels cheap or premium depending on what you hear.

6. Versions for every screen

A modern campaign needs several cuts: horizontal 16:9 for TV and YouTube, vertical 9:16 for Reels and TikTok, square 1:1 for feeds, plus short 6–15 second versions. Planning them from the start saves time later.

Classic shoot or AI?

A classic shoot is right when you need real people performing or a specific real location. AI production is strong when you need worlds that are hard to film — space, history, extreme landscapes, impossible camera moves — or when the product must look perfect without a studio day. Many projects combine both.
