Christmas ads 2026: what this year’s holiday commercials teach brands

Christmas ads 2026 from John Lewis, M&S, Tesco, Sainsbury’s and Aldi: what audiences rated highest, which craft choices worked and what brands can learn.

The Christmas ads of 2026 have arrived in a single rush. Between 1 and 9 November, almost every major UK retailer released its festive film, and audience panels rated them within hours. Together they form the clearest yearly snapshot of what holiday advertising looks like right now — and what any brand planning a seasonal commercial can learn from it.

The season in one week

Tesco and Sainsbury’s opened on 1 November. John Lewis followed on 3 November with its 20th Christmas advert, a three-minute film called Gloria and Pickles, made by Saatchi & Saatchi and directed by Lucy Forbes. Dame Kristin Scott Thomas plays a grandmother who stops putting cash in envelopes after her childhood toy dog, voiced by Paul Whitehouse, comes to life. Sam Fender recorded a new cover of “You Can’t Always Get What You Want” for it.

Aldi launched on 5 November, M&S on 6 November with The Home of Christmas (agency Mother, director Shannon Murphy) starring James Norton as an undercover Santa, and Morrisons on 8 November with a 90-second Goldilocks story that debuts on TV on 9 November.

What audiences rated highest

OnePulse asks 500 UK adults to score each supermarket ad within hours of release, from 1 (“a total turkey”) to 5 (“an absolute cracker”). At the time of writing the ranking is:

  • M&S — 4.0, with 74% saying they liked or loved it and 58% saying it made them more likely to shop there;
  • Aldi — 3.9, with support for bringing back Kevin the Carrot rising from 63% before launch to 73% after viewing;
  • Sainsbury’s — 3.8, voted the most festive;
  • Morrisons — 3.7, the funniest, with 45% saying it made them laugh;
  • Tesco — 3.6, the most emotional, with 28% saying they welled up;
  • Lidl — 3.5 and Asda — 3.3.

The spread is narrow. Every film was watchable; the winners were the ones that gave people one clear feeling — warmth, laughter or a lump in the throat — and tied it to the brand.

Craft choices that stood out

Creative Boom’s round-up shows how varied the production methods are this year. Barbour went to Aardman for stop-motion with Wallace and Gromit. Prada blended hand-drawn animation by John Pomeroy with live action. Sainsbury’s brought back the BFG in a two-minute film with a VFX-built giant world by Electric Theatre Collective. Morrisons cast its Three Bears as animatronic figures, each with its own personality. Retail Gazette notes that Very’s new advert is AI-assisted.

The lesson is not that one technique wins. It is that the method follows the idea: a tactile, handmade look signals care; a fantasy world needs VFX or AI; a comedy needs timing and faces. The audience judges the story, not the toolset.

Story versus price

There is a tension in the numbers. According to OnePulse data cited by Retail Gazette, 57% of shoppers would rather retailers spent less on Christmas advertising and cut prices, and 61% say a Christmas ad has never changed where they do their main festive food shop. Asda leaned into that with a direct value message — and scored lowest on likeability, though its own shoppers rated it more warmly than others did.

Yet the top-rated films also had the strongest effect on intent. M&S had both the highest score and the lowest share of viewers asking for price cuts instead (39%). John Lewis shows the other side: 36% said its advert made them more likely to shop there, but 48% named the dog as the standout element and only 12% mentioned the gifting message unprompted. A memorable film and a memorable reason to buy are not the same thing.

Built for every screen

None of these campaigns is a single TV spot. John Lewis launched first on social, YouTube, Instagram and its website, then on ITV and Channel 4, and runs across video on demand, connected TV, Meta, TikTok, Reddit, Pinterest and Snapchat. Asda’s campaign is four 30-second spots plus a social edit. Sports Direct and Nike paired a 60-second hero film with five 10-second films. Morrisons posted to Instagram three days before its TV debut and planned highlight clips for Instagram and TikTok.

What brands can take from Christmas 2026

  • Choose one emotion. The best-rated ads were clearly funny, festive or moving — not all three at once.
  • Recurring characters build value. Kevin the Carrot and the BFG show that audiences ask for characters to return.
  • Connect the story to a reason to buy. Charm alone does not move people to the shop.
  • Let the idea choose the craft — live action, stop-motion, VFX or AI.
  • Plan the cut-downs from day one: hero film, 30-second spots, vertical 9:16 and 6–10 second clips.
